FULL CAPTION — FACEBOOK / INSTAGRAM / THREADS
==============================

Congress passed two different veterans bills in two days. The foreclosure crisis still isn't over.

On Veterans Day, the House passed TCAVA by voice vote, including proposed changes to future VA disability ratings and higher refinancing fees for some VA borrowers.

On November 12, the House separately passed the modified Major Richard Star Act, 392–18.

Neither bill is law yet.

Meanwhile, GAO's November 10 report confirms VA still lacks a comprehensive plan to evaluate whether its replacement foreclosure-prevention program actually works.

Veterans are still facing higher mortgage payments, foreclosures, and displacement. Families already foreclosed upon don't automatically get their homes back because a new program exists.

The fight isn't just about which veterans receive new benefits. It's about protecting the benefits already earned—and holding the government accountable when its systems fail.

Follow the law. Stop preventable foreclosures. Count the families already harmed. Investigate the decisions.

Sources: U.S. House Clerk, November 11–12, 2026; GAO-27-108908; Veterans Foreclosure Crisis.

==============================
VIDEO SCRIPT — ABOUT 60 SECONDS
==============================

Veterans and families are still losing their homes, and Congress is voting on veteran benefits while the foreclosure crisis continues.

On November 10, the Government Accountability Office released a report on VA's new Partial Claim Program. GAO found VA still does not have a comprehensive plan to measure whether the program actually works. VA gave mortgage servicers until November 28 to implement the updated assistance process. A program announced is not the same as assistance available to a family facing a sale.

On November 11, the House passed the Take Care of America's Veterans Act by voice vote. On November 12, it passed a separate, modified Major Richard Star Act by 392 to 18. Neither vote makes either bill law.

But families already foreclosed upon do not automatically get their homes back. Congress and VA need to preserve records, count outcomes, protect families facing imminent harm where the law allows and examine remedies for those already displaced.

Follow the law. Stop preventable foreclosures. Investigate what happened.
