About this project

This is public-interest documentation, run by affected families, cited evidence, investigative research, fiscal analysis and oversight. It records what happened to veteran families caught between two federal failures: families who took COVID-19 forbearances were left without a workable way back, the replacement built to fix that was terminated on roughly eight days’ notice, and when Congress passed an emergency replacement authority the Department sat on implementation and did not use the additional statutory authority it had been given. The mortgage industry, consumer advocates, national press and members of Congress each warned in advance what would follow. This project documents that foreseeability, and presses for a cohort-wide remedy, benefits reversal, restored eligibility, and remediation for every family left behind. It makes cohort-level demands, never seeks a result in any one family’s matter, and holds itself to published editorial and privacy standards.

Mission

We are veteran families harmed by a foreclosure crisis inside the VA home-loan program, not because anyone stopped paying, but because the government changed the rules underneath them.

This project exists to map the crisis, preserve the record, connect families to vetted help, and press for administrative fixes that already exist in law, without exposing any family to further harm. The full story: What Is Happening · Timeline

Voices in this record

This record is built from more than one family, in more than one state, under more than one servicer

The site is written in one voice. The record behind it is not.

The cohort is weighted toward veterans on fixed VA disability income and their caregivers, for whom a required payment increase was not survivable. What differs is when and how each case failed. Some entered COVID forbearance and were never given a workable exit. Some were denied, or still waiting, when VASP was terminated on May 1, 2025. Some took a modification at a rate they could not carry and are in default again. Some lost the home before the replacement program Congress ordered was finalized on June 1, 2026, and some after, since servicers are not required to offer it until November 28, 2026.

State law then decides what happens after a sale. Washington is non-judicial with no redemption period. Wyoming has one. The same facts, one state line apart, are not the same case.

More than one servicer appears in these files. That is why this reads as a program failure rather than one company’s. Families have continued to come forward through 2026.

This record documents a federal program failure and what it did to the families caught in it

The standards below describe how the record is kept: how claims are sourced, how they are labelled, and how errors are corrected. They are not a statement about who is answerable for what the record shows.

How we handle facts

Editorial standards

The Trust Project standard

This project follows the eight Trust Indicators: who we are, who funds us, how we work, how we source, how our claims are checked, and how mistakes are corrected are all stated on this page or linked from it.

Minimum-source rule

Every public number traces to a primary source with a date. Nothing goes for-the-record on a single unverified figure; unverified items are labeled and held.

The three-layer rule

Documented authority, factual analysis, and community-reported illustration are kept visibly distinct and labeled. See policy.

Content labels

Every claim carries one of: DOCUMENTED FACT, PROJECT ANALYSIS, COMMUNITY-REPORTED, PUBLICLY UNREPORTED, or UNVERIFIED · HELD. Each is defined at Data & Methods →

Never merge the cohort number

The three tiers, 15,000+ foreclosed, 90,000+ behind, 160,000+ impacted, are never collapsed into one figure.

Foreseeability, not intent

The record shows the harm was warned of and proceeded. We never assert intent to foreclose.

Review cadence

Facts carry a last-verified date. Stale figures are framed as stale, not restated as current.

Two lanes, kept strictly separate

Privacy & the litigation firewall

Public / project lane

  • Systemic policy evidence
  • Public records
  • De-identified cohort examples (state + situation)

Personal experience appears only as de-identified illustration. The minor child in any household is never referenced. Consent is recorded before any example is published. See how to share safely.

Naming, and correcting the record

Officials, agencies and servicers are named, and their motives are not

Naming is what an oversight record is. What is not published here is why anyone acted.

This record documents what officials, agencies and servicers said and did, on dates, in venues, measured against the standards they set for themselves. It is not an accusation and it is not softened.

What is not published here is motive. This record does not claim to know what anyone intended, believed or privately decided. Where the record shows an action and a date, the action and the date are what appear. Where the wording of a finding goes beyond a citation, it is labelled as analysis and carries its own limitation, so a reader can see exactly where the document ends and the reading begins.

If you are named here and the record is wrong, it gets corrected. Email veteransforeclosurecrisis@gmail.com with the page and what is inaccurate. Corrections of fact are made on the correction record with a date, on the same standard applied to everything else on this site, and the original wording stays visible.

That standard applies to everyone equally, and it is the standard this project has been asking of the Department for a year.

How this record is built

Every claim traces to a primary document, and the document is reproduced so it can be checked

Four things travel with every claim: an identifier, its source, the date it was last checked, and what it does not prove.

The work behind that is reading hearing transcripts, pulling statutes and circulars, filing records requests, screenshotting agency guidance before it changes, sitting on hold with servicers, and checking one document against another until the timeline holds. It is done by a social worker and MSW graduate student who is also a spouse and caregiver in one of the households documented here.

The limitation on each claim is not a disclaimer. It is what stops a figure being carried further than the document behind it can hold. Registers list every claim and every source, and both are downloadable.

Software is used for the mechanical parts of a project this size. Formatting and structuring pages, turning a register into a table, checking a file for broken links or inconsistent dates, searching a large archive for a document already known to exist, and producing a first draft of writing that is then rewritten. That includes AI tools, and they are named here for the same reason every source is named.

They are not used for:

  • Sourcing. No claim here rests on something a model produced. Every figure traces to a primary document, linked and reproduced.
  • Establishing what is true. Where sources disagree, a person resolves it. Where it cannot be resolved, the page says so and the figure is held rather than published.
  • Producing quotes, dates, statute numbers or citations. These are transcribed from the primary source, and the source is linked.
  • Judgement. What matters, what leads, what a family needs to hear first, and what is fair to say about a named official.

The verification structure is the safeguard, and it is why the registers exist. No claim survives on this site by assertion, from any source. Anything unverified is labelled and held, in public, rather than quietly published. If something here is wrong, this structure is built so a reader can find it, and the correction record shows it when they do.
