The correction record

Corrections & accountability

When we get something wrong in public, we fix it in public. Every correction to a published fact appears here with its date, what it said before, what it says now, and why it changed, so this record can be trusted the way an outlet's record is trusted.

Every correction is timely, explicit, complete and transparent. It is made when the error is found. It says what was wrong. It changes everything the error touched, and it shows the original wording.

This site is now public. This page records material corrections to published facts and interpretations; mechanical fixes remain handled separately under the rule below.

Correction · November 16, 2026 · Watch, policy and fiscal record

Watch. “No final House passage vote is recorded” became outdated when the House passed H.R. 9237 by voice vote on November 11. The July roll call concerned a motion to recommit. The FAIR Veterans Act proposal was on the record in April 2025, before the May VASP cutoff. The revised entry cites the two veterans organizations’ statements on disability offsets separately. The hearing entries now use the documented postponement status. Claims about a substitute event and definitive non-rescheduling were removed. The authority discussion now identifies the separate statutory provisions and H.R. 984’s proposed deadline.

Policy and Current Status. “Between March 2020 and May 2025” now specifies March 1, 2020 through May 1, 2025. The criteria comparison does not establish how many borrowers each condition excludes. “$147M net savings vs. the cost of foreclosure” misdescribed the bill-wide CBO forecast. The new copy gives the scope of the bill and provision scores and identifies the separate VFC household scenario.

Sources and Methods. The former C001 limitation said no authoritative public VA foreclosure series had been located. It now identifies GAO’s quarterly national series of completed VA-guaranteed foreclosures through June 2026 and preserves the separate date and limits of the hearing statement. See Watch, Policy, Current Status, and Methods and source records.

Correction · 2026-11-15 · VA foreclosure reporting

Earlier wording: Watch described the national foreclosure count as unpublished and called family records the only running count. Methods omitted the national VA series.

Corrected wording: GAO published national quarterly completions through June 2026 using VA data. VFC has yet to verify a comparable monthly series with local breakdowns.

Why it changed: Table 6 of the November 10 GAO report documents completions. The table does not report VA eviction filings or servicer-level Partial Claim outcomes. See Watch and Methods.

Correction · 2026-11-15 · HUD-VASH referrals

Earlier wording: We reported that 174,045 veterans were left without a HUD-VASH referral between 2020 and 2024.

Corrected wording: GAO counted 174,045 non-referral instances. VA recorded no reason in 151,296 instances (87%).

Why it changed: GAO's report says a veteran could have more than one intake form. We corrected Current Status, Policy, Fiscal, Watch, and source S012.

Correction · 2026-11-15 · June 2026 VA and FHA pooled-loan comparison

Earlier wording: We reported 1.23% for VA and 0.42% for FHA, a ratio of 2.97.

Corrected wording: We counted 946 foreclosure-related pool removals per 77,306 seriously delinquent VA loans (1.22%) and 1,655 per 405,336 seriously delinquent FHA loans (0.41%). The ratio is 3.00 using the unrounded figures.

Why it changed: The previous calculation divided removals from all pools by delinquency counts from Ginnie Mae II alone. Both sides now cover Ginnie Mae I and II. Dividing a monthly count by a snapshot of delinquent loans cannot establish a borrower's foreclosure risk or the effect of the Partial Claim policy. See Current Status and source S242.

Correction · 2026-11-09 · M26-4 Chapter 22 a.5 / rebuttal PR-02

Earlier wording: The barriers page described criterion a.5 as excluding "anyone already in the foreclosure process," and PR-02 treated the provision as a blanket foreclosure bar.

Corrected wording: VA says loans referred to foreclosure by their mortgage servicer can still be reviewed for a VA Partial Claim. Criterion a.5 disqualifies loans facing foreclosure initiated by another lienholder, such as an HOA; borrowers must still meet other conditions.

Why it changed: The final handbook text was read too broadly. VA's published servicer FAQ (S025) directly resolves the distinction. The rule is still agency guidance rather than text expressly imposed in 38 U.S.C. § 3737, and other barriers remain subject to the project's separate analysis. Corrected on Barriers and PR-02.

Correction · 2026-11-09 · Accountability tracker

Earlier wording: Mission Roll Call and the National Consumer Law Center were listed as publicly opposing the home-loan funding-fee offset based on housing/suicide and mortgage-servicing testimony, and the American Legion's broader support for TCAVA was not shown alongside its earlier Section 4 objection.

Corrected wording: Those testimony sources document separate housing and loan-servicing concerns, not a specific offset position. The American Legion's current support for the broader TCAVA package is stated with its earlier Section 4 comments. VFW and Common Defense objections retain their own dated citations.

Why it changed: A citation must support the particular public position attributed to an organization. See the reconciled tracker and the Legion's published TCAVA position.

Correction · 2026-10-29 · Claim C031

Earlier wording: “The bill that creates the VA partial claim also eliminates judicial review of VA's decision to take a secured interest in a veteran's defaulted home loan.”

Corrected wording: “The enacted VA Home Loan Program Reform Act makes VA decisions under 38 U.S.C. § 3732(a)(2) final and not subject to judicial review.”

Why it changed: The earlier register relied on a CBO description of H.R. 1815 and had not verified the enacted provision. Pub. L. 119-31 § 2(a)(1)(B)(iii) added 38 U.S.C. § 3732(a)(2)(C), limiting judicial review of decisions under that paragraph. The law separately provides in § 3737(f)(2) that individual Partial Claim decisions are final and not judicially reviewable. These are distinct provisions; neither statement establishes that all claims against a mortgage servicer or all challenges to general agency policies are barred. The claim register and Methods table now distinguish the enacted authority from the CBO estimate.

Correction · 2026-10-29 · Claim C061 / rebuttal PR-19

Earlier wording: “Two of the ten qualifying criteria were changed or added after the public comment window closed, so the people who commented commented on a different document.” The prior public explanation also said the prior-COVID-assistance exclusion was absent from the February draft.

Corrected wording: “Between the February draft and June final handbook guidance, VA replaced the draft's six-payment re-entry after a modification with a flat 24-month lockout; the prior-COVID-assistance restriction was already present as draft criterion 9 and was retained as final a.10.”

Why it changed: Review of the February draft found that its criterion 9 already included prior assistance through a § 3737 Partial Claim, the COVID-19 Veterans Assistance Partial Claim, or a COVID Refund Modification. The final retained that restriction as a.10. The change established by this comparison is in a.7: six monthly payments since the most recent modification became a 24-month lockout. We corrected claim C061, How the Rule Changed, and the affected rebuttal explanations. The underlying exclusion remains consequential; it was not newly introduced after the comment period.

Correction · 2026-09-18

A factual review of Current Status corrected several statements that went further than their sources supported. The earlier 160,000+ campaign total was removed because the component populations overlap and the overlap has not been established; C003 is retained as PROJECT ANALYSIS only to document that corrected historical arithmetic and must not be used as an unduplicated total. The 15,000+ figure is attributed to the Senate hearing statement; the ~90,000 figure is dated to its April 2026 report; the Dec. 12, 2024 warning is no longer presented as documented fact while its source is pending; the VA/FHA comparison no longer implies causation; the ~31,500 active-foreclosure figure remains a VFC-derived estimate with its calculation shown; and the 58,000+ figure is cited to the organizations that reported it rather than described as VFC-derived subtraction. On September 22, the claims register and remaining public 160,000+ uses were reconciled with this correction.

Working notes written while the site was being built are not corrections and are not kept on this page. They are held in the project archive.

Mechanical fixes are handled differently. Spelling, punctuation, a broken link, a typo that changes nothing about a claim: fixed without a note. Anything that changes what a reader would take away, a figure, a date, a name, a characterisation of what someone said or did, gets a dated entry here, every time.

We correct errors we find ourselves, not only the ones reported to us. Figures carry a last-verified date and are re-checked; a check that finds an error makes a correction on the same terms. This record is kept as one list so that the pattern of errors, not only each one, is visible.

Retraction is a last resort. Removing part of the record destroys the ability to check what was said and when, which is the opposite of what this site is for. Material is removed only in three circumstances: removal is legally required; the flaws are serious enough that no correction repairs them; or the verification process was not followed before publication. If anything is retracted, this page will say what it was, when it went, and why. The absence will not be silent.
