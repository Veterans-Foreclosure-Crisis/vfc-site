Congress Passed the Foreclosure Relief. The VA Was Warned. Veterans Are Still Losing Their Homes. Why?

VA Secretary Doug Collins ended VASP in May 2025, and Reps. Mike Bost and Derrick Van Orden supported its termination. Congress authorized Partial Claims in July 2025. VA launched the replacement in June 2026; servicers have until November 28 to implement it. Families already foreclosed or displaced still lack a guaranteed remedy.

VA issued its final policy on June 1 and launched on June 15. The 180-day implementation period is measured from the June 1 final policy, not from the June 15 launch. These dates establish the implementation window. They do not establish the current status of every servicer.

The Partial Claim exists. Who can actually get it?

Congress enacted the program's statutory framework. VA's handbook sets additional conditions, and each servicer must implement the program. A family must clear both barriers before relief can reach its loan. Read the eligibility analysis.

The Partial Claim is live at VA; access depends on whether the borrower's servicer has implemented the new waterfall.

PROJECT ANALYSIS

One program does not reach every affected family

This project's analysis finds three groups of families, each harmed by a different government action, and no single program that reaches all three.

COHORT A

The forbearance families

Who
Families who did exactly what the government said and paused payments under COVID forbearance.
What changed
The same-rate exit disappeared when the COVID partial claim ended in October 2022. Pushed toward balloon payments or unaffordable modifications.
The fix they need
A restored same-rate modification or partial claim, applied to families already forced into higher-rate deals.

COHORT B · THE VASP GAP

Foreclosed in the coverage gap

Who
Families who fell into default after the VA killed VASP (May 2025) and before the Partial Claim launched (June 2026), a year with nothing operational to help.
Unresolved
Many are already foreclosed on or displaced.
The fix they need
The unused Section 3(h) authority, applied to pre-enactment defaults, plus counting and rehousing the displaced.

COHORT C

Eligible on paper, barred in practice

Who
Families who may qualify under the new rules but are blocked by VA-added eligibility criteria, or are still waiting for their servicer to complete implementation during the rollout window.
The fix they need
Remove the administrative barriers, and pause foreclosures and evictions until the program is operational at every servicer.

What this section does not establish

The three cohorts are a project analysis, not an official classification, and they are not added together into a single total.

WHAT MUST HAPPEN

THE PROJECT'S ASKS

  1. Pause & count

    Pause VA foreclosures and evictions until the new relief is actually working at every servicer, and publish the monthly counts. The VA has used a pause before; members of Congress and the National Consumer Law Center have already asked for it again.

  2. Remove the handbook barriers

    The barriers excluding already-harmed families were not written by Congress into H.R. 1815, the VA Home Loan Program Reform Act. VA added them in handbook text and can revise them the same way.

  3. Count the displaced

    Publish the monthly VALERI aggregates, new defaults, foreclosure starts, and VA-acquired REO, and build a remediation pathway for families already foreclosed, past sale, or displaced during the implementation gap. The policy is forward-only and leaves that cohort with no remedy. The mandated GAO review arrives after the harm, not during it.

  4. Use the authority already given

    Congress expanded the Secretary’s authority in 38 U.S.C. §3737(h) in November 2025. It permits interim guidance and gap-period relief before regulations issue, and it sits unused.

  5. Investigate the decisions — and the continuing human harm

    Obtain the VA, servicer and congressional records behind the VASP shutdown, rejected or unused interim protections, eligibility restrictions, postponed oversight, implementation and foreclosure outcomes. Require VA and its partners to report whether families are still in crisis, homeless, or trapped in unstable housing or new poverty; how foreclosure and displacement affected mental health, suicide prevention, care coordination, spouses, caregivers and children; and what VA, HUD and community programs actually did to prevent and respond to that harm. Document outcomes without exposing private families.

  6. Account for families already harmed — including possible return of homes

    For VA-acquired homes not yet transferred to new owners, demand an immediate legal, title, financing and inventory review of options to return the property to affected veteran families. Examine restoration of VA home-loan entitlement, appropriate correction of inaccurate foreclosure-related credit reporting, closing-cost and fee relief, affordable financing in today's market, and coordination with available VA-owned properties and housing-support programs. These are requested remedies to investigate, not rights or payments already guaranteed by current law. Include families whose homes were sold, who accepted short sales, or who remain displaced.

VA's June 2026 handbook policy is final, but servicer implementation continues through November 28, 2026. Existing law authorizes important intervention and early guidance; mandatory retrospective restoration, fee waivers and remedies for completed sales may need additional legal authority, funding, consent or case-specific review.
