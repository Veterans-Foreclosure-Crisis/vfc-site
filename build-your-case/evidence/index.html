PROJECT ANALYSIS C034

The money in VA’s rules does not point toward keeping families in their homes.

A successful loan modification pays the servicer $700 at the top tier under 38 C.F.R. § 36.4319(b), while CBO records that VA typically pays lenders up to 25 percent of the outstanding mortgage balance when a borrower’s home is foreclosed upon, up to $75,000 on a $300,000 balance. This project reads that gap as an absence of financial pull toward retention, and as a reason not to rely on servicer initiative to reach families already in default.

eCFR Title 38 Part 36 (VA loan regulations) ↗ CBO score, H. Rept. 119-104 Sec. 3 ↗

Limitation: This is our reading, not rule text. The incentive is paid to the servicer and the guaranty payment to the holder, which are often but not always the same company, and a guaranty payment reimburses a documented loss rather than producing profit. It is not a finding that any servicer profits by foreclosing.

Last verified 2026-07-25

DOCUMENTED FACT C035

VA told Congress in February 2024 that it could not offer a partial claim, and explained the risk-share reason why.

In written testimony to the House Veterans’ Affairs Subcommittee on Economic Opportunity, VA’s Executive Director of Loan Guaranty Service stated that unlike HUD and USDA, VA “does not have explicit authority to advance guaranty funds as a loss mitigation option” and “cannot offer a partial claim that pulls from the amount VA guaranteed”; that it had been VA’s “longstanding and public position that Congress never authorized a partial advance on the guaranty without termination of the loan”; and that the lack of authority “may be because VA only carries 25% of the risk of a default through its guaranty, where the other Federal programs carry 90% or more of default risk.” The same statement records that the COVID-VAPCP Program expired October 28, 2022, and that the program “began to incur a cost for each new guaranteed loan commitment.”

Written statement of John Bell III, VA Loan Guaranty Service, HVAC 2/15/24 (source URL being pinned)

Limitation: This is VA’s position as of February 2024, before Congress created the permanent partial claim in Pub. L. 119-31 (July 30, 2025). It explains the pre-2025 gap; it is not a statement about VA’s authority today.

The Urban Institute’s July 2024 study states that “the VA has the most limited loss mitigation options, lacking both a portfolio and partial claim authority,” and recommends that “Congress needs to grant the VA partial claim authority and allocate funding to establish a more robust loss mitigation waterfall framework similar to what GSE and FHA borrowers currently access.” It also records that VA acquired partial-claim funds in July 2021 and that those funds expired in October 2022, leaving the gap that ran until Pub. L. 119-31.

Urban Institute, Preventing Foreclosures: How the Pandemic Reshaped the Loss Mitigation Toolkit (7/2024) ↗ Written statement of John Bell III, VA Loan Guaranty Service, HVAC 2/15/24 (source URL being pinned)

Limitation: Independent analysis, not a government finding. The paper names two dates in the same paragraph, partial-claim funds acquired July 2021 and expired October 2022, and the COVID-19 Refund Modification “operated on and off after the VA partial claim authority expired in July 2022”, reflecting the run of short extensions rather than a single end date. This site uses VA’s own date for the COVID-VAPCP expiry, October 28, 2022 (S052).

DOCUMENTED FACT C039

Analysts noted at the time that VA could have built a more flexible modification under the authority it already had.

On VASP’s flat 2.5 percent rate, the Urban Institute wrote that “the VA, with its existing authority, could have introduced a more flexible modification rather than using the flat 2.5 percent for all modifications,” and set out an alternative targeting a 20 to 25 percent payment reduction with a 2.5 percent floor, which would have “reduced the cost to the government.” The paper also records that for most VA borrowers a standard loan modification raises the monthly payment, because prevailing rates exceed origination rates.

Limitation: This concerns VASP’s design in 2024 and speaks to the scope of VA’s existing discretion. It is not a statement about the June 2026 partial-claim policy, whose ceilings Congress set in statute.

DOCUMENTED FACT C064

VA Circular 26-19-22 requires the lender to twice present the veteran with a comparison of the two loans, once within three business days of application and again at closing.

DOCUMENTED FACT C072

A family whose servicer modified their loan in the previous 24 months is barred from the partial claim by a rule Congress never wrote, and the clock runs to a date the servicer controls.

M26-4 Chapter 22, final, effective June 1 2026, criterion 22.02 a.7, verbatim: The borrower has not received a loan modification in the 24 months preceding the date the servicer evaluates the loan under the Waterfall. The lookback therefore ends on the date of the servicer's evaluation, not on any date the family controls. 38 U.S.C. 3737(a) sets three conditions and contains no lookback of any length and no reference to a prior loan modification. The February 2026 draft had no such criterion; its criterion 7(a) asked only that a modified loan have six monthly payments since the most recent modification.

VA Servicer Handbook M26-4 Chapter 22, VA Partial Claim, FINAL, effective June 1 2026 ↗ Pub. L. 119-37 §7307(e); 38 U.S.C. §3737(h) as amended ↗ VA Servicer Handbook M26-4 Chapter 22, VA Partial Claim, DRAFT ↗

Limitation: Compares handbook text with statutory text. It establishes that the lookback is an agency addition and that its end date is set by the servicer, not that it is unlawful.

DOCUMENTED FACT C073

Congress made the loss mitigation sequence mandatory; VA's handbook makes the foreclosure halt wait until a trial payment plan is offered.

38 U.S.C. 3732(d), added by Pub. L. 119-31 section 2(a)(3) on July 30 2025, verbatim: The Secretary shall prescribe loss mitigation procedures, including a mandatory sequence in which the holder of a loan guaranteed under this chapter shall offer loss mitigation options (including an option to enter into a partial claim agreement under the VA Home Loan Program Reform Act) to a veteran, to help prevent the foreclosure of such loan. The Secretary may not purchase an entire such loan until the veteran has completed such sequence. It names no trial payment plan. M26-4 Chapter 22 section 22.03 a.2, final, verbatim: Servicers must cease initiating, continuing, or completing foreclosures on loans where a TPP is offered to the borrower. Section 22.03 a: Servicers will only offer a TPP when the qualifying criteria are met. So the halt is conditional on all ten criteria.

38 U.S.C. 3732, Procedure on default, subsection (d), loss mitigation sequence ↗ VA Servicer Handbook M26-4 Chapter 22, VA Partial Claim, FINAL, effective June 1 2026 ↗

Limitation: Quotes the statute and the handbook. It establishes what triggers the halt on paper; it does not establish what any servicer did in any case, and it does not assert that the handbook is unlawful.

DOCUMENTED FACT C074

VA's partial claim chapter recognises a spouse when the marriage ends or the borrower dies, and not while the household is intact.

M26-4 Chapter 22, final, effective June 1 2026, searched in full on 2026-08-21: the chapter contains no occurrence of the words co-borrower, caregiver or household. It addresses family circumstance only as exceptions to a rule written about one borrower: a former spouse who received the property through a divorce (22.02 a.8(a)), a surviving spouse who received it from the estate (a.8(b)), a Successor in Interest who assumed the loan before the default (a.8(c)), a borrower deployed, hospitalised or in long-term care, where occupancy is met if the property is a family member's primary residence (a.3(c)), and a co-obligor whose signature is waived because they committed domestic violence (a.9(d)). Eligibility itself is decided on one borrower and that borrower's residence.

VA Servicer Handbook M26-4 Chapter 22, VA Partial Claim, FINAL, effective June 1 2026 ↗

Limitation: A stated negative: three words are absent from one chapter, verified by a full-text search of the final. It is a finding about the program's drafting, not about how any servicer applies it, and not about any one family. It does not assert that a caregiver spouse on the note is excluded in practice, only that the chapter never names the arrangement.
