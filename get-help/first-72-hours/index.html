GET HELP · THE FIRST 72 HOURS

The first 72 hours

If you are behind, in foreclosure, or facing eviction.

Take it one step at a time. You are not alone, and you have more options than a servicer’s letter suggests. Work down this list in order. The first two steps are the most time-sensitive.

IF YOU ARE IN CRISIS

988, then press 1

Or text 838255. The Veterans Crisis Line is available around the clock.

DO THIS FIRST, IF YOU HAVE A SALE DATE OR EVICTION NOTICE

Do not sign anything, including a “cash for keys” offer, before you speak to a legal-aid attorney. A pending sale can sometimes be paused, but timing matters. Call a legal line below today.

WHICH KIND OF STATE ARE YOU IN

One question changes your timeline more than any other. Does a court have to approve the sale where you live? Find your state’s answer before you rely on any date, including the ones on this page. The legal-aid line below will know it.

NO COURT HEARING

A trustee, a third party named in your loan papers, can sell the home without a judge. Washington is one of these states. Loans like this are usually a deed of trust, and the sale is called a trustee sale. Timelines are short. Start today.

COURT HEARING REQUIRED

The lender files a lawsuit and you are served with court papers. You get a place to answer, and usually more time. Answer by the date printed on the papers. Missing that date can end the case without you.

BOTH KINDS No one can remove you without a court order. If a sale happens, the new owner has to file a separate eviction case, called an unlawful detainer or UD. A sale date is not a move-out date.

WORK DOWN THIS LIST IN ORDER

  1. Reach the right help for your stage today

    Sale date or eviction notice: call legal aid or a foreclosure attorney today; timing can change what is still possible.

    Home not sold, trying to keep it: call a HUD-approved housing counselor at 1-800-569-4287 and ask for your state foreclosure-prevention or mediation program.

    Housing instability, displacement, or rehousing: call SSVF at 1-877-424-3838 and ask about homelessness prevention or rapid re-housing.

    Local food, utilities, rent, and resource discovery: dial 211.

  2. Gather your documents

    • COVID forbearance approval letter
    • Every modification offer (rate + new payment)
    • All servicer letters & denial notices
    • Your original payment & rate
    • Any sale-date or eviction notice

  3. Start a call log

    For every call, write the date, who you spoke with, and what they said. Save every letter, email, and voicemail. Your records may be the only running account of what happened, they matter.

  4. Ask your mortgage company (servicer)

    Questions about assistance and your payment

    You can copy these questions into a message to the company that handles your mortgage payments.

    • “Can I apply for the VA Partial Claim through your company now? If not, when will you begin accepting applications?”
    • “Is this rate at or below VA’s posted maximum for modifications this week (the Freddie Mac weekly rate plus 0.5%), and no more than one percentage point above my current rate? Both limits apply. Can you offer a lower rate?”
    • “If the offer exceeds either limit, what VA approval applies, and was it obtained before entering into the modification agreement? Please provide the approval and its date in writing.”

    Get answers in writing.

    Questions about foreclosure or a scheduled sale

    Your paperwork may use different names. Foreclosure can proceed through a court (“judicial foreclosure”) or through a process outside court (“nonjudicial foreclosure”). In some states, the auction is called a trustee’s sale. A notice using that term still concerns foreclosure; you do not need court papers for the risk to be real. How foreclosure works.

    Questions you can copy and send:

    • “Have you started foreclosure proceedings on my loan? Is a foreclosure sale, including a trustee’s sale, scheduled? Please provide the current status and any sale date in writing.”
    • “Is my application for mortgage assistance complete? What date did you receive the complete application? If anything is missing, please list exactly what you need.”
    • “While you review my application, which foreclosure steps are paused, and which are continuing? If you are continuing, please explain the basis in writing.”

    What this means for your family

    Foreclosure during an assistance review. “Dual tracking” means the mortgage company is moving toward foreclosure while also reviewing you for help keeping your home. Federal rules prohibit certain foreclosure actions when the required conditions are met. Protections depend on factors including when your application became complete and how close the foreclosure sale is; not every simultaneous review and foreclosure activity is automatically unlawful. Read the federal foreclosure-review protections.

    If you receive foreclosure papers or a sale notice: Do not assume an assistance application has stopped the foreclosure. Keep the notices and written responses, and contact legal help promptly about the deadlines.

    For modifications made without prior VA approval, 38 C.F.R. § 36.4315(a)(8) sets both rate tests. The survey benchmark is the most recent Freddie Mac U.S. weekly rate for 30-year fixed conforming mortgages on the modification approval date, rounded to the nearest 0.125 percentage point, plus 0.5 percentage point. A special rule applies to certain government-assisted loans. Paragraph (b) requires prior VA approval if a condition in paragraph (a) is not met; this section does not guarantee a modification.

  5. File to create a record

    You can file with the CFPB, the VA Inspector General, your State Attorney General, and your member of Congress. Filing builds a record and can prompt casework on your behalf.

  6. You are not the first

    Other veteran families have been through this and triage new arrivals every week. Find the community and the full resource list at veteransforeclosurecrisis.org, and share this page with anyone who needs it.

One thing to remember

A “cash for keys” offer asks you to give up your home, and sometimes your rights, for a payment. Never sign it, or any settlement, before a legal-aid attorney reviews it with you.
