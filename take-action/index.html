Eight Actions VFC Is Asking Congress And VA To Take Now

These eight requests use different mechanisms: some ask VA to exercise existing powers, some require congressional investigation, and some may need legislation or funding. Veterans facing imminent sale, higher-payment modifications and completed foreclosure need different solutions.

PROJECT ANALYSIS

1

Ask 1 · Pause & Count

Request a lawful, targeted pause on VA-guaranteed foreclosures while eligible borrowers cannot actually access assistance, and review VA-controlled eviction actions separately. NCLC and 28 House members requested interim protections; the VA must identify the legal basis, covered loans, timing and limits. A congressional request alone does not stop a foreclosure sale.

NCLC: pause until accessible ↗28-member letter led by Rep. Pappas ↗

VA protection request — scope to verify

2

Ask 2 · Use Lawful Authority, Review Exclusions

Use 38 U.S.C. § 3737(h) to issue lawful implementation guidance where permitted, and identify which exclusions are statutory versus administrative. Review independent foreclosure-prevention and property powers under §§ 3732 and 3720 without promising automatic eligibility. For families already displaced, identify whether VA or a third party actually holds title, then examine any lawful review, resale, lease or compensation route; some remedies may require new legislation or funding.

H.R. 1815 (the authority) ↗

Existing powers are conditional; other remedies may need legislation

3

Ask 3 · Publish The Counts

Direct the VA to publish monthly counts of VA-loan foreclosures and VA-as-plaintiff eviction filings, plus servicer-level partial-claim uptake, denial, and compliance data. Congress cannot verify the launch narrative without the numbers, and families should not be the only ones keeping score.

VA data and congressional oversight request

4

Ask 4 · Clocks & IG Oversight

Fixed processing clocks and Inspector General oversight of servicer systems, now proposed for loan assumptions, should reach loss mitigation and partial-claim processing, where the harm is happening now. Audit VALERI on the affected loans while you are there. A servicer may have offered a modification while still failing to transmit a required VALERI event; both accounts can be true. Reconcile the servicer’s complete loss-mitigation file, dated offers and borrower notices against VA’s event logs, rejected submissions and approval records. Identify the applicable duty and what the original records actually establish.

Existing IG review; new clocks may need legislation

5

Ask 5 · No Pay-Fors Off Veterans

Remove the disability rating reductions and home loan fee increases from pending legislation, and reaffirm that veterans organizations may oppose legislation without retaliation against their accreditation.

Congressional amendment and vote needed

6

Ask 6 · Warm Referrals To Housing

Warm referrals to veteran-friendly landlords, property managers, and real estate professionals, and case-management capacity that can walk displaced families from intake to lease. This is the immediate, practical need every week.

What this actually asks of you →

Voluntary housing referrals; capacity varies

Ask 7 · GAO Investigation

Request a GAO follow-up on VA’s Partial Claim evaluation plan, the VASP-to-Partial-Claim transition, servicer reporting, affordability and completed foreclosure outcomes. GAO’s November 10, 2026 report found VA lacked a comprehensive evaluation plan. Separately examine HUD-VASH referral findings as a different program and evidence question; do not imply a proven common mechanism without a matched investigation.

The non-implementation pattern, on Policy →

GAO oversight request; new audit scope to define

8

Ask 8 · The Dexter Amendment, Senate Side

Co-sponsor or vote for a Senate-side version of Rep. Maxine Dexter’s Amendment #60 V2 to H.R. 8469, which pairs the Secretary’s travel-budget withhold with VA Circular 26-24-12, the moratorium framework VA Loan Guaranty designed in May 2024, and with Partial Claim Program implementation under 38 U.S.C. § 3737. It was not made in order under H. Res. 1275 and remains available as a Senate model.

What happened at Rules →

Proposed Senate mechanism, not enacted

New evidence for these requests: GAO’s November 10, 2026 Partial Claim review found VA lacked a comprehensive program-evaluation plan. VA gave servicers until November 28, 2026 to implement the program. Neither milestone by itself establishes that a particular veteran received relief or addresses every completed foreclosure.

These requests require different decision-makers. VA can exercise some existing powers, while Congress may request records, legislate or pursue oversight under applicable rules. A congressional letter is not a foreclosure stay; proposed measures are not enacted law. Compare those tools and their limits at Instruments that compel on the oversight record.

What Congressional Offices, Committees, VSOs And Housing Providers Can Do

What moves this, and who moves it

Member Offices: Casework

When a veteran foreclosure case reaches your district office: open a constituent inquiry to the VA and confirm it stays open until a substantive written response arrives (at least one cohort inquiry was administratively closed with no response sent). Request the VALERI record and the servicer’s complete loss-mitigation file. Do not accept “modifications were offered and declined” without requesting dated offers, applicable policy terms, approval or exception records and the borrower’s response. Refer the family to this project for peer navigation, and to their state AG and state DFI for parallel complaints.

Member Offices: Legislation

Co-sign or renew the moratorium demand (Ask 1); back the counting requirement (Ask 3), the cheapest ask here and the hardest to argue against; extend processing clocks and IG oversight to loss mitigation (Ask 4); and insist any benefits package drops the rating cuts and fee increases (Ask 5).

Committee Staff: Oversight

Three document productions resolve most factual disputes: (1) VALERI records for loans foreclosed January 2025 to present; (2) servicer loss-mitigation files and borrower-disclosure documents for forbearance exits; (3) the VA’s internal counts of foreclosures and VA-as-plaintiff eviction filings (Ask 3). Family witnesses remain prepared; recorded testimony is available to any committee that will take it.

VSOs

Add the foreclosure cohort to your legislative agenda alongside the benefits fight (Asks 1 and 5). They are the same members. Offer accredited-representative time for the benefits consequences that follow foreclosure-driven collapse. Share this brief with service officers so families calling about foreclosure get routed, not turned away. Your housing and emergency-assistance funds reach further paired with this project’s intake.

Landlords, Property Managers & Neighbors

The single highest-impact act available to anyone reading this (Ask 6): agree to take warm-referral calls for screened veteran families whose credit damage traces to this documented federal failure, and say so to this project. A list of willing landlords, growing one name at a time, is currently the difference between housed and homeless. What this asks of you, in detail →

Ask 6 · Help Displaced Veteran Families Connect To Available Housing

Foreclosure-damaged credit can block the next lease even when a family can afford the rent

Some displaced veteran families can document sufficient income yet face additional rental-screening barriers after foreclosure; other households need income or rental assistance. If you own a rental, work in real estate, or simply know someone who does, that is the gap you can close.

This is the only ask on this site that does not require a member of Congress, a regulator, or a lawyer. It requires one person deciding to take a phone call.

How Foreclosure-Damaged Credit Blocks Rental Applications

When a foreclosure damages credit, the harm does not end at the sale. A family may be able to afford rent and still fail an automated screening check; another may need both housing assistance and a landlord willing to review the full circumstances. Those screening results rarely show whether affordable mortgage relief was unavailable, denied or inadequate. For some families, the foreclosure that caused displacement can also block the next lease. A person can look beyond what an algorithm leaves out.

Verifiable income does not guarantee rental access. Some displaced families have VA benefits or wages but face foreclosure-related screening barriers; others also need income support, rent assistance or both. VFC's analysis connects these downstream barriers to gaps in federal mortgage relief, while each family's circumstances and available remedies need individual review. PROJECT ANALYSIS

Why The Networks Are Not There

There is a public assumption that veteran families have deep support networks, and that a disabled veteran family in particular has an organisation standing behind them. In this cohort that has generally not held. Households organised around caregiving are frequently down to one income and one driver, with the caregiver’s own work and social life narrowed for years. Medical relocations, deployments and service moves cut the local ties most people rely on. By the time the foreclosure arrives, the family often has fewer people to call than a neighbour with no service connection at all would have. PROJECT ANALYSIS

This is why a warm handoff works where a resource list does not. A list assumes someone to call. The handoff supplies the call.

If You Own Or Manage A Rental

The ask is bounded, and it is not charity:

  • Take one referral call. Not a commitment to rent. A conversation with a family this project has already spoken with.
  • Look at the file, not the number. Verifiable income, rental and payment history before the foreclosure, and a written explanation of what happened, with the dates and the federal record behind it.
  • Say what would make it work. A larger deposit, a co-signer, a shorter initial term, a landlord reference from the prior mortgage servicer. Families would rather hear a condition than a no.
  • If it is not a fit, say so, and say why. A specific reason is worth more than a polite decline, because it tells the next family what to fix.

If You Do Not Own A Rental, You Are Still The Person Who Can Do This

This is the part that gets skipped, and it is the part that has actually worked. Most housing in this country is not held by large management companies. It is held by individuals, and individuals are reached through the people who already know them.

  • Ask the landlord you already have. A current or former landlord who knows you is a warmer introduction than any cold application.
  • Ask the agent who sold you your house. Real estate agents and property managers know who holds rentals in a given town, which is exactly the knowledge a displaced family does not have and cannot buy.
  • Ask in the rooms you are already in. A union hall, a congregation, a small-business group, a neighbourhood page. One post asking “does anyone here rent out a house” reaches further than a family applying cold to twenty listings.
  • Offer to be the reference or the first call. Vouching costs nothing and is often the whole difference.

What This Project Does On Its Side

Families are spoken with before any referral. What a landlord receives is the household’s own verifiable income, its rental and payment record before the foreclosure, and a plain written account of what happened with the documentary record behind it. No case numbers, no minor children, nothing a family has not agreed to share. Anyone who prefers to be a name on a list without taking calls can be exactly that.

Scripts · Video, 60 Seconds

Sixty-second video script

A one-minute script you can read to camera, written for someone hearing about this for the first time.

Quick thing you need to know. By May 20, 2026, a Senate hearing had placed veteran home losses above 15,000. Newer September estimates include higher figures, but they do not reconcile into one authoritative current VA count. Here's what happened. During COVID, veterans were told to use forbearance, pause their mortgage payments. When forbearance ended, the VA had a partial claim program that let them catch up at their original rate. Then the VA cancelled it without notice. FHA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac all kept their programs. Only the VA pulled the rug. So the VA built a replacement called Veterans Affairs Servicing Purchase (VASP). It worked. 17,000 families enrolled. Then the VA killed that too. May 2025. No replacement ready. That's the second time veteran families had a rescue program cancelled out from under them without notice. Congress saw the pattern, two administrations had now done this to veterans, so they passed a permanent fix, H.R. 1815, with bipartisan support, in the House under suspension of the rules, which takes a two-thirds majority. Both chambers passed it by voice vote and no one asked for a recorded vote. Signed into law July 2025. VA launched the Partial Claim on June 15, 2026, but servicers have until November 28, 2026 to implement the new Waterfall and Partial Claim together. Launched is not the same as available at every servicer. The implementation question now is whether a family's own servicer has the new VA loss-mitigation path operational before foreclosure proceeds. More than 90,000 VA borrowers were reported behind on their loans or in the foreclosure process in April 2026. That is a dated distress snapshot, not a current foreclosure-loss count. The published affected-population counts overlap, so VFC does not add them into a single total. This isn't left vs. right. Both administrations contributed. Congress already fixed the law. The VA is the bottleneck. Call (202) 224-3121 and tell your representative to demand the VA pause all veteran foreclosures and evictions until the program is live.
