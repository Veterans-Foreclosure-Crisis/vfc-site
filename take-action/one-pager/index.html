Veterans Are Still Losing Their Homes

VA mortgage-relief changes across two administrations affected veteran families at different stages. The timeline shows when those decisions happened, who was harmed, and why families still need different remedies.

Problem 1. VA Eligibility Rules Under Review

Congress authorized a five-year Partial Claim Program and required VA to prescribe a loss-mitigation sequence. VA’s M26-4 Chapter 22 adds eligibility and processing conditions, including default status, current-servicer requirements and trial plans. VA should identify which are statutory and which it may lawfully change administratively. 38 U.S.C. § 3737(h) permits guidance in advance of regulations for specified pre-enactment defaults; it cannot override the statute or undo a completed foreclosure sale.

Problem 2. Access Still Depends on the Servicer

VA began accepting Partial Claim trial-plan events June 15, 2026. Servicers have until November 28 to implement the new waterfall. VFC has not verified a complete public servicer-by-servicer implementation list. Access must be verified with the borrower’s own servicer. S025 A program can be live at VA and still unavailable on a particular loan.

The Fiscal Cost of Failed Foreclosure Prevention

Cost of preventable loss, not realized savings: NCLC cited about $72,000 in average guarantor loss per home disposition; CRL estimated about $73,750 for VA. Auction.com estimated that roughly 75% of foreclosures of VA-guaranteed loans lead to VA-owned REO (March 2026 hearing submission; not a verified VA-wide rate). CBO's May 2025 H.R. 1815 score projected $294 million in avoided guarantee costs, $170 million in net direct-spending savings from Partial Claims (section 3), and $147 million in net direct-spending savings for the entire bill over FY2025–2035. These are projections, not observed results.

What VFC Is Asking Congress and VA to Do

1. Pause foreclosures & evictions

Halt VA-guaranteed foreclosures and the evictions the VA files as plaintiff until the Partial Claim is operational at the veteran’s own servicer.

2. Remove the barriers & identify the harmed.

Identify which M26-4 Chapter 22 barriers VA can lawfully revise; use § 3737(h) guidance where permitted. Publish foreclosure data and separately examine help and remedies for families whose homes were already sold.

3. Rehouse & make whole

Coordinate rapid rehousing and restorative options for families’ loss of home, equity, and stability, case management, not a referral hotline.
